Privacy policy

Last changed 25 November 2026.

Postrinch is a desktop mail and calendar app made by LBME Publishing ("we"). This page says what Postrinch does with your information, and what this website does.

In short: we don't store any of your data, not on our servers and not in any cloud: we have no servers. Postrinch runs on your computer and keeps your mail there. We never receive your mail, your calendars, your contacts, your passwords or your sign-ins, and we collect nothing about how you use the app.

What Postrinch reads, and where it keeps it

  • Your Google mail and calendars. When you add a Google account, Postrinch asks Google for your account's email address (openid, email), so it knows which account signed in, and for permission to read, organise and send its mail (https://mail.google.com/), to see and change its calendars (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar) and, optionally, to make Gmail filters for your rules (gmail.settings.basic). It downloads your mail, labels and events from Google and stores them, with a search index of them, in your own home folder.
  • Your other mail accounts (Fastmail, iCloud, a work server: any account with IMAP and SMTP). Postrinch signs in to the account's incoming (IMAP) and outgoing (SMTP) servers, downloads its folders and mail into your home folder, and sends your mail through its outgoing server. A Microsoft account (Outlook.com, Hotmail, Live, Microsoft 365), once Postrinch offers Microsoft's sign-in, signs in on Microsoft's own page and asks permission to read and change its mail over IMAP (IMAP.AccessAsUser.All), to send over SMTP (SMTP.Send), to stay signed in (offline_access) and to learn the account's address (openid, email). A Google account added over IMAP asks Google for its mail and address alone (https://mail.google.com/, openid, email). Other accounts sign in with the password, or app password, you give.
  • Your sign-ins. The tokens Google and Microsoft give Postrinch, and the passwords you give it, are kept in your system's keychain (GNOME Keyring or KDE Wallet on Linux). Each is sent only to the provider or mail server it belongs to, and no agent you connect ever sees one.
  • Your Thunderbird settings, only when you choose Import from Thunderbird: Postrinch reads the account settings in your Thunderbird profiles on this computer (addresses, names and servers) to list them for you. It doesn't read Thunderbird's saved passwords, and nothing it reads leaves your computer except to sign in to the accounts you choose.
  • Your settings (views, rules, label colours, which senders' pictures to show, which agents you've connected) are files in your home folder.

Postrinch uses this information only to show you your mail and calendars and do what you ask with them: list, search, sort, file, send, remind and notify. It doesn't use it for advertising, doesn't sell it, and doesn't use it to train AI models.

Who Postrinch talks to

  • Google, to sync your mail and calendars and to carry out what you do in the app: send a message, archive a thread, answer an invitation.
  • Your other mail providers: Microsoft, to sign in to a Microsoft account, and each account's mail servers, to sync its mail and carry out what you do.
  • Where your mail servers are listed, when you add an account by its address: Postrinch looks for the servers in your mail domain's own settings file (asked of your mail domain, with your address, as Thunderbird does), in Mozilla's public directory of mail providers (autoconfig.thunderbird.net, which is told only a domain: yours, such as example.com, or that of the server that receives your domain's mail), and in your domain's public DNS records. Failing those, it connects to the usual server names at your domain (such as imap. and smtp.) to see which answer, and servers that your domain's DNS records name elsewhere are shown to you to confirm before any password is sent. Accounts imported from Thunderbird use Thunderbird's servers and skip this.
  • GitHub, once a day, to check for a new version of Postrinch and download it. GitHub sees your computer's address and that it asked; nothing about your mail is sent. Postrinch 0.1.34 and earlier also sent GitHub your own GitHub sign-in with that check, if you had one on the computer (the POSTRINCH_GITHUB_TOKEN setting, or the GitHub command line tool signed in). Postrinch 0.1.35 and later send none.
  • The senders of mail you open, only when you choose "Show pictures": your computer then fetches that message's pictures from where the sender put them, as any mail app does. Postrinch sends no cookies or referrer with those requests, and pictures stay off until you allow a sender.
  • Agents you connect. If you connect an AI app such as Claude, Cursor or Codex in Settings › Agents, that app can read and act on the mail and calendars of the accounts you give it, through Postrinch on your computer. What it reads then goes to that app and its provider, under their terms, including whether they may use it to train their models: check those terms before you connect one. You choose each agent and its accounts, and you can remove it at any time. An agent can't send mail or tell anyone about an event without you.

Postrinch sends nothing to LBME Publishing, has no analytics, and reports no crashes or usage.

How long it's kept, and how it's protected

Your mail and calendars stay on your computer for as long as the account is in Postrinch, kept up to date with each account's provider, and are deleted when you remove the account. They are files in your home folder, with the same protection as the rest of it; Postrinch doesn't encrypt them itself, so use your system's disk encryption if your computer may be lost or shared. Sign-in tokens and passwords are in your system's keychain, which does encrypt them. Connections to Google, Microsoft and GitHub are encrypted (HTTPS), and Postrinch never signs in to a mail server without encryption (TLS).

Google user data

Postrinch's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Information from Google is used only to provide the features of Postrinch you see and use. It is transferred to others only to provide those features at your direction (such as to an agent you connect), to comply with the law, or as part of a merger or sale with notice to you. No person at LBME Publishing reads it, because it never reaches us.

Removing your information

  • Remove an account in Settings › Accounts: Postrinch deletes its token or password from your keychain and its mail and calendars from your computer, and for a Google account also withdraws its access at Google. Your mail on the server is not touched.
  • Withdraw Postrinch's access to a Google account at any time from your Google Account's permissions, and to a personal Microsoft account from your Microsoft account's app permissions. For a work or school Microsoft account, ask your organisation's administrator.
  • Remove everything: first remove each account in Settings › Accounts (that clears your keychain and withdraws access at Google; withdraw a Microsoft account's as above) and each agent in Settings › Agents (that takes Postrinch out of the agent apps' settings). Then close Postrinch and delete its mail and settings (~/.local/share/postrinch, ~/.config/postrinch), attachments you opened (~/.cache/postrinch), and the program: ~/.local/share/postrinch-app, the links ~/.local/bin/postrinch, postrinch-cli and postrinch-mcp, the menu entry ~/.local/share/applications/postrinch.desktop and its icons, ~/.local/share/icons/hicolor/*/apps/postrinch.*.

This website

postrinch.jkbase.app sets no cookies and has no analytics or advertising. Its host keeps ordinary request logs (address, page and time) to run the site. The download section asks GitHub's public API which release is the latest, from your browser, so GitHub sees that request; the downloads themselves come from GitHub.

Children

Postrinch is not directed at children under 13, and we collect no information from anyone.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be here with its date. A change that lets Postrinch send anything to us, or anywhere new, will be said in the app before it happens.
