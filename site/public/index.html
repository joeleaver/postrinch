Your mail, on your computer, open to your agents.

Postrinch is a fast desktop mail client for Gmail and, soon, any IMAP account, written in Rust, with your calendar beside it and search that runs on your own machine. Everything you can do in the window, Claude can do too, through MCP, and nothing that leaves your account goes without your say.

Mail that keeps up with you

Postrinch keeps your mail and its index on your computer, so lists open at once, search answers as you type, and it all works on a train.

Search for what you rememberComing

You remember what an email was about, not the words in it. Ask for "the email where someone offered to cover my shift" and Postrinch will find it by meaning, with a language model that runs on your computer. Your mail never goes to a search service.

Today, search covers every account at once, by words, people, labels, dates and attachments, with the filters you know from Gmail. Words alone search 64,000 messages in tens of milliseconds. Mail that hasn't downloaded yet is asked of Gmail and listed below.

Every account, one inbox or many

Add as many Gmail accounts as you have and see them side by side, or together in one inbox. Fastmail, iCloud, Outlook and your work server join them over IMAP soonComing, with the same views, search, rules and agents.

The sidebar holds views, not labels: saved searches like "everything from my bank, any account" or "unanswered for a week". The inbox keeps Gmail's tabs. Read in conversations, or one message at a time.

Archive, label, star or trash and it happens at once. Postrinch tells Gmail in the background, in the order you did things, even after a day offline.

Rules that run where they should

One set of rules per account: label it, file it, flag it, forward it, or ask an agent to summarise it.

A rule runs on Gmail when Gmail matches exactly the same mail, so it works while your computer is off, and in Postrinch when Gmail can't. You never have to think about which.

Your calendar beside your mail

Day, week and month of every Google calendar you have. Drag an event to move it; Postrinch asks before it tells anyone.

Invitations show as a card in the mail, with what clashes, and you answer Yes, Maybe or No right there. Share a calendar, or add one someone shared with you.

Built for agents, with you in charge

Every action the window offers is a tool an agent can use. Connect Claude, Cursor or Codex from Settings › Agents in one click.

  • Read, search, file and label mail; keep your views and rules.
  • See your calendars, find you free time, add and move your own events.
  • Write a reply or a new message, and answer an invitation, as a proposal you send or not.

An agent can't send mail, tell anyone about an event, or see a password. Each sees only the accounts you give it, and mail reaches it marked as text written by strangers.

And the rest

The parts you notice only when another mail app gets them wrong.

Mail that can't follow you

HTML mail is cleaned before it's drawn. Pictures from the web stay off until you allow a sender, so tracking pixels don't fire, and a link shows where it really goes.

Notices you choose

New mail by view, not by account. Event reminders as Google Calendar sets them. Quiet hours, with reminders let through or not.

Updates you can trust

It checks for a new version daily, verifies its signature, and waits for you to choose "Restart to update".

Sign-in that stays yours

You sign in to Google on Google's own page, in your browser, so Postrinch never sees your password. What it keeps to stay signed in lives in your system's keychain, out of every agent's reach.

Its own database

Mail, people, labels and views are stored and searched by rhypedb, an embedded database in Rust that runs inside Postrinch, not beside it.

Windows and macOSComing

Linux first. The same app, packaged for the others, is next.

Written in Rust, drawn by Rinch

Postrinch is built with Rinch, a GUI framework for Rust. Rinch lays a window out with HTML and CSS and draws every pixel itself, in Rust, with no Electron and no web view: one small program that starts in a moment. When something changes, such as an unread count or a new message, Rinch updates just that part of the window.

The same engine draws your HTML mail. Each message is cleaned first, then laid out by Rinch, so reading mail never means running a web browser inside your mail app.

Yours, on your computer

There is no Postrinch server. Your mail, its index and search live on your computer, and your sign-ins are kept in your system's keychain, out of every agent's reach. Postrinch talks to your mail provider, to GitHub for its daily update check, to a sender's server only when you choose to show their pictures, and to the agents you connect. Nothing goes to us. The privacy policy has the details.

Download for Linux (x86-64)
  • Signature · release key
  • All releases

64-bit x86 with glibc 2.39 or newer (Ubuntu 24.04, Fedora 40, Debian 13 or later), GTK 3, libxdo (the libxdo3 or xdotool package), and a keyring: GNOME Keyring or KDE Wallet.

  1. Unpack and install. It installs into your home folder and your app menu, no root needed: 
    mkdir postrinch && tar -xzf postrinch-<version>-linux-x86_64.tar.gz -C postrinch
./postrinch/postrinch --install
  2. Set up Gmail sign-in. For now, Postrinch signs in with a Google client you make yourself: ten minutes, step by step.
  3. Add your accounts in Settings › Accounts, and connect an agent in Settings › Agents if you like.
Check the download's signature 
openssl pkeyutl -verify -rawin -pubin \
  -inkey postrinch-update-key.pem \
  -in postrinch-<version>-linux-x86_64.tar.gz \
  -sigfile postrinch-<version>-linux-x86_64.tar.gz.sig

The same key is built into Postrinch, which refuses any update it doesn't verify.

Why your own Google client? Google lets a new mail app sign in only a few listed testers until it has passed Google's review. Until Postrinch has, a client of your own puts you in charge of your own sign-in, and your mail still goes only between your computer and Google.
